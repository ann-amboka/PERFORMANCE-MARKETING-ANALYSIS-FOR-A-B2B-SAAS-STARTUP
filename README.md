# PERFORMANCE-MARKETING-ANALYSIS-FOR-A-B2B-SAAS-STARTUP
This is a detailed marketing project for a startup looking to enter the market in Q1 2027; it is a detailed strategy I developed, the setup of the META ADS, and a performance analysis
Tallybridge: Go-to-Market, Meta Ads, and Performance Analysis

A 90-day market-entry case study for a Canadian B2B SaaS startup. It covers the client acquisition strategy, the Meta Ads campaign setup, and the performance analysis, ending with a plan to reach 80 paying customers.

Please read first: Tallybridge is a fictional company built for this case study. All performance figures in this project are simulated sample data used to demonstrate the analysis method. They are not results from a real campaign.

1. Business context

Tallybridge captures, codes, approves, and pays supplier invoices. It is built for Canadian taxes (GST/HST, PST, QST), Canadian payment rails (EFT, Interac e-Transfer), English and French, and Canadian data residency.

Pricing (CAD per month)

Starter: 149 (up to 100 invoices)
Growth: 399 (up to 500 invoices)
Scale: 899 (up to 2,000 invoices)
Enterprise: custom

Main challenges at launch

No Canadian brand awareness and only a few referenceable customers
A trust-heavy sales cycle averaging about 52 days
No accounting-firm referral channel yet
A small team and a limited marketing budget
2. Client acquisition strategy
Ideal customer profile
20 to 200 employees
150 to 2,000 supplier invoices per month
CAD 5M to 60M annual revenue
Using QuickBooks Online or Sage 50, often with manual email and spreadsheet processes
Priority industries: construction and trades, wholesale and distribution, professional services and engineering, multi-location franchises and hospitality
Five steps
Define the ICP using company size, invoice volume, revenue, and current systems.
Identify 200 prospects, 50 per priority industry, with decision-makers (CFOs, controllers, finance managers, owners, COOs).
Run personalized email outreach, tailored to each industry's invoice challenges.
Run LinkedIn outreach through existing and secondary connections, with optional short founder-led videos to build trust.
Run paid advertising, starting with a $1,000 Meta Ads test.
Four acquisition channels
Channel	Approach
Outreach	Audience-led (connections, referrals) and method-led (cold email, LinkedIn DMs)
LinkedIn content	Posts on manual invoice processing, delayed approvals, tax-coding errors, and reconciliation
Paid advertising	Days 1-30 Meta awareness, days 31-60 retargeting, days 61-90 Google Search
Community engagement	Finance and business communities to build credibility and find referral partners

4. Measurement framework
Metric	Formula
CPM	Spend / Impressions x 1,000
CTR	Clicks / Impressions
CPC	Spend / Clicks
CPL	Spend / Leads
CPA	Spend / Customers
ROAS	Revenue / Spend
EPA	Revenue / Customers
APL	Revenue / Leads
APC	Revenue / Clicks

Decision targets (assumptions to tune): CTR of at least 1.0%, CPL of at most CAD 70, and CPA at most the budget divided by customers needed.

5. Performance analysis (simulated data)

Each period is reviewed in three parts: what happened, was it good or bad, and the recommendation.

Days 1-30: Initial Meta campaign ($1,000)
What happened: 733 clicks and 15 leads at about $67 per lead. No customers yet.
Good or bad: Good. CTR and CPL met their targets.
Recommendation: Scale the accounting firms ad set (lowest CPL) and pause the weakest ad set.
Days 31-60: Retargeting and prospecting ($5,000)
What happened: Retargeted website visitors, video viewers, and page and ad engagers. Added lookalike audiences and testimonial-based prospecting. Result: 68 leads and 3 customers.
Good or bad: Mixed. CTR was fine, but CPL and CPA were higher than desired.
Recommendation: Use evidence from the 6 design partners to build trust, cap ad frequency on small retargeting audiences, and move budget to the best performers.
Days 61-90: Google Search and value-based retargeting ($10,000)
What happened: Added Google Search Ads and a value-offer retargeting campaign. Result: 134 leads and 17 customers.
Good or bad: Volume improved, but CPA stayed above target.
Recommendation: Lead with a free invoice cost audit, waived onboarding fees, and a guided 14-day trial. Put most of the budget on the best performers.

6. The gap to 80 customers

The gap

Paid media produced 20 customers in the sample.
If all 6 design partners convert to paid, the total is 26.
That leaves 54 customers short of the 80 target.

Required CPA

A $16,000 budget needs a blended CPA of about $216 to reach 80.
The sample CPA is $800, roughly 3.7 times too high.

What each lead-to-customer rate would demand (74 customers needed after design partners convert)

Lead-to-customer rate	Leads needed	Max CPL allowed	Budget needed at current CPL
10%	740	$22	$54,562
15%	493	$32	$36,375
20%	370	$43	$27,281
25%	296	$54	$21,825
35%	211	$76	$15,589

What closes the gap

Paid media alone is unlikely to reach 80 at the observed acquisition cost.
Partner referrals (accounting firms), direct outreach, and early conversion of the free design partners are needed alongside ads.
With a 52-day average sales cycle, leads from late in the window may not close by day 90. A guided 14-day trial can speed this up, backed by fast follow-up.
